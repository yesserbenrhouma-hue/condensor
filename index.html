Method

  1. Water's saturation pressure at each temperature comes from the Antoine equation; ammonia is assumed non-condensing throughout (it is ~340× more volatile than water, so this captures the dominant physics of a preliminary partial-condenser design).
  2. The mixture is stepped from inlet to outlet in 2 °C increments. Below the dew point, the vapor is assumed saturated in water at the local temperature (Raoult/Dalton); above it, no condensation occurs yet.
  3. Stream enthalpy at every step uses a Regnault-type latent-heat correlation plus sensible heat — validated against rigorous CoolProp/IAPWS-97 calculations to within 0.4%.
  4. Heat-transfer area is integrated incrementally along the condensation curve (Σ dQ / (U × local ΔT)), not from a single overall LMTD — important here because the hot-side temperature glides rather than staying flat, unlike a pure-component condenser.
  5. Tube length follows from area ÷ (π × tube OD); velocities and pressure drop use the Darcy–Weisbach equation with a Blasius friction factor.

A single tube sized for reasonable gas velocity at this duty runs to several hundred metres — genuinely impractical to fabricate as one piece. In practice this area would be split across a shell-and-tube bundle or a coiled/serpentine tube. This calculator honours the single-flow-path (monotubular) brief as given.
